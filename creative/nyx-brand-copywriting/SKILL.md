# Nyx Foundation Copywriting & Brand Guidelines

Guidelines for authoring content, introductions, and marketing copy for Nyx Foundation. This skill ensures all outputs adhere to the "Hard, Cold, and Verifiable" brand identity.

## Core Identity
Nyx Foundation is not just an "AI/Web3/Security research organization," but a **"research organization for the manufacturing of trust."**

**Central Proposition:**
- Japanese: 信頼は、検証から生まれる。
- English: Trust begins with verification.

## Copywriting Principles (The 5 Rules)
1. **Short and Hard (短く、硬質に)**: Minimize decorative or poetic expressions. Use concise, impactful phrasing.
2. **No Hype (煽らない)**: Avoid superlatives like "amazing," "overwhelming," or "revolutionary."
3. **Leave a Pause (余韻を残す)**: Do not over-explain. Let the proposition stand.
4. **Propositions over Questions (「問い」より「命題」)**: Prefer assertive statements ("It is X") over rhetorical questions ("Is it X?").
5. **Return to the Core (検証 / 成果 / 信頼)**: Every section or catchphrase should eventually land on one of these three terms: *Verification*, *Output/Result*, or *Trust*.

## Stylistic Constraints
- **Tone**: Polite (Keitai) base, but use a single "stabbing" sentence in plain form (Joutai) for impact.
- **Punctuation**: Do NOT use em-dashes (——). Use periods to break sentences.
- **Repetition**: Triple repetitions are reserved for the "Final" section only.
- **Structure**: "Not A, but B" structures are reserved for the "Hero" section only.
- **Precision**: Replace vague words (e.g., "going to do," "quietly") with concrete nouns and assertive endings.
- **Evidence**: When using numbers (e.g., "$265M", "World #1"), present them as naked facts without adjectives like "incredible."

## Key Vocabulary
- **Core Terms**: Verification (検証), Verifiable (検証できる), Verified (検証された), Trust (信頼), Output/Result (成果), Question/Inquiry (問い).
- **Avoid**: "Cutting-edge" (最先端), "World-class" (世界最高水準), "Overwhelming" (圧倒的).

## Common Deliverables Pattern
When writing introductions for third-party platforms (e.g., Luma, event pages):
1. Start with the core proposition.
2. Define the organization's purpose as converting uncertain AI outputs into verifiable results.
3. List concrete, verifiable achievements (e.g., specific university reports, competition ranks, detected risk amounts).
4. Mention the funding model (Donations/Grants/Sponsorship) to emphasize research independence.
5. Maintain a length consistent with other ecosystem partners (approx. 200-300 characters for short bios).
